This heritage viewer provides quick lookup functionality for exploring
the historic environment record. Here we answer some key questions.

## What is the purpose of this viewer?

To make public historic environment information quick and easy to
access on a range of devices, as a gateway to more powerful research tools.

## What are the different dots on the map?

When zoomed close in, blue map markers point to any search results.

When zoomed out, large circles indicate search matches instead. When you have
a selected record type, small grey circles represent entries that do not
match your search, to help provide context.

While blue markers clearly indicate a precise location, they can be
confusing when large numbers appear at once. When zoomed out, the map may
show hundreds of visible matches, so larger dots are used for performance
and clarity. Clicking a dot will zoom to that location and show blue markers.

## What data does this contain?

This viewer contains a subset of heritage data. Much more is available
through the full Arches-based research tools.

## Why can I not see all entries at once?

This service aims to be quick, robust and simple. Rather than risking
poor performance on older devices, it requires some filtering - by text,
by record type and/or by zooming. This ensures we do not load large
amounts of data unexpectedly.

## What technologies does this use?

This uses technology from the [Arches Project](https://www.archesproject.org/)
ecosystem. The platform is an [AGPL-licensed](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/agpl-3.0.html)
tool called **Starches**, which combines Hugo, Pagefind, Alizarin, Flatgeobuf
and OpenFreeMap to create a performant, fully-static map service.

## Can I contribute?

If you are a technologist interested in contributing to the underlying code,
please take a look at the Arches Project and Starches repositories. As with
all open source contribution, please note license requirements and reach out
before spending significant time on pull requests.
